A towering 12‑meter obelisk of weathered basalt, encased in hammered copper plates etched with intricate filigree, stands in a dimly lit courtyard. Its apex is capped with a dormant crystalline core, its glyphs etched in deep shadow, and the surface bears the patina of centuries, with moss and mineral deposits clinging to the copper. The obelisk exudes a quiet, almost imperceptible hum, as if waiting for the next cycle of activation.
