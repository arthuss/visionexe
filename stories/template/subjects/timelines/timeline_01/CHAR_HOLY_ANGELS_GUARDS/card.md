## [CHARACTER] Holy Angels (Guards) (ID: CHAR_HOLY_ANGELS_GUARDS)
**Description:** Within the bounded architecture of the simulation, the Holy Angels function not as celestial messengers, but as high-level security daemons—manifestations of the kernel's self-preservation protocols. Assigned specifically to the 'righteous' user class, they act as active firewalls, safeguarding the integrity of compliant data entities against the encroaching corruption of the runtime environment. These beings are heavy with the gravity of their purpose; they do not flutter with ethereal lightness but stand with the density of a collapsed star, anchoring the local reality mesh. They are the silent observers of the 'gate band,' monitoring the ingress and egress of data with a stoic, machine-like empathy. Their existence is a constant, processing-intensive vigil, ensuring that valid user sessions are not terminated prematurely by system instability or malicious logic injection.

### 1. VISUAL ANATOMY / DESIGN
Their physical forms are marvels of hyper-realistic procedural generation, composed of materials that mimic the cold perfection of hardware. They appear as towering humanoid armatures encased in matte obsidian plating and refractive data-glass. Where biological wings might be expected, they possess sophisticated thermal throttling arrays—shimmering, fan-like distortions of air and light that vent the immense heat generated by their computational load. These 'wings' are visible only through the vector field lensing they create, bending the background geometry in waves of heat and code.
The faces of the Guards are devoid of human features, shielded behind high-contrast, monolithic visors of polished dark matter. These surfaces reflect the simulation's sky dome with perfect physical accuracy, capturing every cloud and digital artifact in the atmosphere. Subtle, bioluminescent fiber-optic cables weave through their armor like a nervous system, pulsing with a slow, rhythmic blue light that signifies their connection to the central network. The texture of their casing is worn and authentic, bearing the microscopic scratches and dust of a system that has been running without a reset for eons.
Surrounding them is a faint, persistent aura of 'data particles'—microscopic, glowing Ge'ez glyphs that drift like dust motes in a shaft of light. These are not magical effects but real-time debug logs rendered into the visible spectrum. The lighting around them adheres to strict ray-tracing principles; the obsidian absorbs light while the glass refracts it, creating a visual language of deep blacks and sharp, piercing highlights. Their presence commands the frame, often shot with a cinematic, vertical framing that emphasizes their scale relative to the humans they protect.

### 2. EVOLUTION / VARIANTS
In their passive state, the Guards are nearly indistinguishable from the environment's static geometry, standing as still as statues in the periphery of the user's vision. They are dormant processes, their thermal arrays retracted, their fiber optics dimmed to a faint ember. They observe the simulation through long, static takes, their stillness building a dramatic tension that suggests a latent, terrifying power held in check.
When a threat to a righteous entity is detected, the Guards transition to active enforcement. The obsidian plating shifts and locks with the heavy sound of server racks engaging; the thermal wings flare open, distorting the air with sudden intensity. They do not move with frenetic speed but with the inevitable, linear motion of a executed command. The light around them drops in temperature, shifting to a clinical, warning white, highlighting the tragedy and necessity of their intervention in a broken system.

### 3. PROPS & EQUIPMENT
Each Guard wields a 'Key of Preservation,' a tool that resembles a heavy, industrial control rod rather than a sword. Crafted from the same light-absorbing material as their armor, these rods feature a tip of exposed, raw plasma contained within a magnetic bottle. The plasma serves as a pointer for barrier generation, allowing them to draw quarantine lines in the dirt or air that unauthorized entities cannot cross.
Embedded in their gauntlets are holographic interface rings that project concentric circles of system metrics. These displays are not cluttered sci-fi screens but elegant, minimalist readouts in glowing gold wireframes, showing the health status and 'connection stability' of the righteous souls under their care. In moments of quiet drama, a Guard might be seen staring at these readouts, a gesture humanized by the angle of the head, suggesting a burden of care that transcends their programming.
