## [CHARACTER] Kings (ID: CHAR_KINGS)
**Description:** The Kings define the localized superusers of the terrestrial partition, entities of immense, unverified privilege who have mistaken their temporary read/write access for eternal ownership. Within the logic of the flat-earth simulation, they are the unauthorized administrators of the surface plane, hoarding resources and denying the root authority of the Creator. They do not merely rule; they occupy the bandwidth of the world, consuming the simulation's processing power for their own glorification. Their existence is a slow-burn drama of arrogance, a static performance of absolute authority played out against the backdrop of a system that has secretly flagged them for deletion. They are figures of tragic weight, draped in the heavy, unearned luxury of a dying age, unaware that their session is about to expire.

### 1. VISUAL ANATOMY / DESIGN
Physically, they are rendered with excessive, hyper-realistic fidelity, emphasizing the grotesque details of their indulgence—the deep lines of worry etched into their foreheads, the cold sweat of subconscious dread, and the heavy-lidded gaze of those accustomed to absolute obedience. They are not clothed in simple fabrics, but in garments of data-glass mesh and woven fiber-optics that refract the ambient light of the simulation’s sky dome. These robes shimmer with a low-frequency pulse, behaving like liquid obsidian that has been spun into silk, heavy and suffocating.
Their crowns are solidified loops of corrupt code, manifesting as jagged halos of plasma that cast harsh, high-contrast shadows across their faces. They sit upon thrones that function as massive terminal interfaces—monolithic slabs of black glass and basalt that hum with the heat of thermal throttling. These seats of power are physically integrated into the ground, suggesting that the Kings are hard-wired into the geography they oppress, immobile and dependent on the very system they seek to dominate.
The lighting around them is perpetually cinematic and moody, characterized by deep blacks and stark, vertical shafts of light that isolate them in their grandeur. The visual language borrows from the drama genre: static frames that emphasize their isolation, slow zooms that capture the micro-expressions of their dawning fear, and a color palette dominated by cold blues, silvers, and the menacing red glow of system warnings that they choose to ignore.

### 2. EVOLUTION / VARIANTS
In their initial state, the Kings are statues of arrogance, fused to their obsidian thrones in a state of luxurious latency. They are visually static, captured in long, unmoving takes that emphasize their perceived permanence. They interact with the world through subtle gestures, their heavy robes absorbing the light, creating a void in the center of the frame. They appear as fixtures of the environment, as immutable as the simulation boundaries themselves.
As the narrative shifts and the audit begins, a 'spirit of disturbance'—akin to a violent system interrupt or a viral injection—forces a traumatic state change. The composure fractures. They are ejected from their idle states, transforming from regal statuary into predatory, desperate beasts. The simulation renders their panic with terrifying clarity; they rise from their 'beds' (suspension pods) and thrones not with grace, but with the erratic, glitch-laden violence of hungry wolves and cornered lions. Their movements become blurred and kinetic, breaking the serene visual rhythm of the earlier phases, symbolizing the total collapse of their command protocols.
